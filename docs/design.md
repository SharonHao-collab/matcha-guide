# Beginner Matcha Drink Guide

## Goal

Help a beginner make one enjoyable iced matcha latte at home when they want a refreshing break.

## Small Starting Point

The first version will teach only one drink: a lightly sweetened iced matcha latte.

One serving uses:

- 1 level teaspoon (about 2 g) matcha powder
- 1/4 cup (60 mL) hot water, about 175°F / 80°C
- 3/4 cup (180 mL) cold milk or oat milk
- About 1/2 cup ice
- 1 teaspoon maple syrup, optional

The learner will make one serving, taste it, and make one adjustment if needed.

## Intended Behavior

When a beginner follows the guide, they should be able to:

1. Identify the required and optional ingredients.
2. Measure the ingredients.
3. Mix matcha with hot water until mostly smooth.
4. Add milk, ice, and optional sweetener.
5. Taste the drink and describe it.
6. Adjust the drink to suit their preference.

## Expected Behavior

The learner completes the drink without outside help. The drink has no large lumps, and the learner can decide whether it is too strong, weak, bitter, milky, or sweet.

The learner uses a simple rule to improve it:

- If it is too strong or bitter, add a little milk or sweetener.
- If it is too weak or milky, use a little more matcha next time.
- If it is lumpy, sift the matcha and whisk it more carefully next time.
- If it is too sweet, use less sweetener next time.

## Actual Behavior at the Start

The learner may know that matcha is powdered green tea and that it mixes with water. However, before using the guide, they may not yet know:

- which ingredients are required;
- how much of each ingredient to use;
- the correct order of the steps;
- how to prevent lumps;
- how to improve the drink after tasting it.

These are starting assumptions. They must be checked by observing a beginner use the guide.

## Expected and Actual Behavior Gap

| Area | Expected behavior | Possible actual behavior to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ingredients | Selects matcha, water, milk, and ice | Is unsure which ingredients are essential |
| Measurement | Uses the listed amounts | Guesses or misreads an amount |
| Sequence | Mixes matcha and water before adding milk | Adds everything at once |
| Texture | Produces a mostly smooth drink | Leaves dry matcha lumps |
| Taste | Describes the result clearly | Says only “good” or “bad” |
| Adjustment | Chooses a suitable change | Does not know what to change |

## Learning Test

Give the guide to one person who has not made matcha before. Do not coach them during the test.

Observe whether they can:

- finish the drink using only the guide;
- follow the steps in order;
- identify one taste or texture problem;
- choose an appropriate adjustment;
- explain what they would change next time.

After the test, ask:

1. Which step was unclear?
2. How would you change the drink if it were too bitter?
3. Could you make it again without help?

## Evidence of Learning

The learner has learned the behavior when they can independently make the drink and make a sensible adjustment based on its taste or texture.

A stronger sign of learning is that they can repeat the process several days later with little or no help.

## Computational Thinking Concepts

- **Decomposition:** Break the task into ingredients, mixing, assembly, tasting, and adjustment.
- **Sequence:** Complete the steps in the correct order.
- **Conditionals:** Use “if this happens, then make this change” rules.
- **Iteration:** Taste, adjust, and try again.
- **Testing:** Watch a beginner use the guide and compare the result with the expected behavior.
- **Debugging:** Find the unclear step or failed result, then improve the instruction.

## First Success Criterion

One beginner can use the guide to make an iced matcha latte, describe the result, and select one appropriate adjustment without help.

## Out of Scope for the First Version

- Traditional matcha ceremony techniques
- Multiple matcha recipes
- Advanced tools or latte art
- Detailed matcha grading and sourcing
- Nutrition or health claims
